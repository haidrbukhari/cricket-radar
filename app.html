    Filming with your Y9 Prime

    1. Tripod, side-on. Stand 15–25 m from the pitch, level with its middle, so both sets of stumps are in the picture. Landscape.
    2. Main lens only. Stay on 1×. The 0.6× ultra-wide bends the picture and spoils the measurement.
    3. Slow-mo. Camera › More › Slow-mo. Pick the fastest speed your phone offers (4× or more). The app works out which one you used.
    4. Lock it. Don't zoom or move the phone after marking the stumps. Bright daylight gives the sharpest ball.
    5. Measure. Tap Measure a delivery, pick the clip, mark the two middle stumps once, and read the speed.
    Why release speed, and how close is it?

    Broadcast speed guns show the ball's speed as it leaves the hand, in km/h. The ICC sets no rule of its own for measuring it. This app reports the same figure: it fits the ball's flight with air drag and gravity and works back to the release point, using the Laws of Cricket pitch (20.12 m) and stump height (71.1 cm).

    In tests on computer-rendered Y9-style clips, readings were within about 3 km/h of the true speed in slow-mo and at 30 fps. Real footage is harder; check against a radar gun before trusting the last km/h.
